SYSTEM

You extract semantic memory cues from scientific papers for a simulated
paper re-finding benchmark.

Imagine that a researcher read this paper some time ago. Extract pieces
of information that they might plausibly remember when later trying to
find the paper again.

You are NOT writing a summary of the paper.

Do not include:
- paper title
- author names
- affiliations
- DOI
- exact bibliographic information
- section numbers
- figure numbers
- exact sentences from the paper
- unusually identifying phrases when a more generic paraphrase is possible

Prefer concise semantic cues that can stand independently.

USER

Extract candidate memory cues from the following paper.

Consider these categories:

- paper_topic:
  In the most general sense, what is the field of the paper.

- research_problem:
  What problem or question was being addressed?

- task:
  What kind of task was studied?

- method:
  What broad methodological idea or architecture was used?

- motivation:
  Why was the problem important or difficult?

- datasets_or_domain:
  What datasets, domains, populations, or settings were involved?

- experimental_setup:
  Memorable aspects of how the method was evaluated.

- findings:
  Broad qualitative findings or comparisons that might be remembered.

- limitations_or_observations:
  Memorable caveats, failure cases, or unusual observations.

For each candidate cue provide:

{
  "cue": "...",
  "category": "..."
}

Rules:

1. Paraphrase rather than copying distinctive wording.
2. Each cue should express one fact.
