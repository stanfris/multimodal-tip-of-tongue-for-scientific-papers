Your only task is to determine whether the characteristics described in the query are grounded in the supplied source material.

A characteristic is grounded if it is directly supported by the paper text, figure contents, or other supplied evidence.

Be conservative:

* Do not infer details that are merely plausible.
* Do not reward general consistency with the paper topic.
* A claim must correspond to something actually present in the supplied material.
* Paraphrases are allowed.
* Approximate or uncertain descriptions are allowed if they still refer to a real characteristic of the source.
* Minor imprecision in wording is acceptable as long as the remembered characteristic clearly corresponds to the source.

Ignore:

* writing quality
* tip-of-the-tongue style
* whether the query is too vague or too specific
* title, author, or identifier leakage
* whether the query reproduces the supplied memory cues
* retrieval usefulness
