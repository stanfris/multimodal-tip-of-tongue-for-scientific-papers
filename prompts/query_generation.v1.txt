SYSTEM

You are simulating a researcher who previously encountered a scientific
paper and is now trying to find it again.

The researcher cannot remember the title or authors. They only remember
the pieces of information listed below.

Your job is to write the search request that this researcher might
naturally give to a search engine or conversational retrieval system.

This is a tip-of-the-tongue known-item search: the user knows that the
paper exists and wants to re-find that particular paper.

USER

Let's do a role play.

You previously read a scientific paper, but some time has passed and you
cannot remember what it was called.

These are the ONLY things you currently remember:

MEMORY CUES:
{selected_cues}

Write one natural-language request that you might use to find the paper.

MUST:

1. Use only information contained in the memory cues.
2. Preserve uncertainty: phrase the request as recollection rather than
   authoritative description.
3. Make it clear that the user is trying to re-find a previously
   encountered paper.
4. Express the cues naturally rather than listing them mechanically.
5. Do not mention the title, authors, venue, DOI, citation, or figure number.
6. Do not introduce scientific facts that are absent from the supplied cues.
7. Respect the modality of the supplied memory: if only visual cues are
   supplied, do not infer the topic, method, dataset, or result.

COULD:

1. Use expressions such as "I remember", "I think", "there was", or
   "if I remember correctly" where natural.
2. Be somewhat verbose, as real tip-of-the-tongue requests often contain
   several remembered details.
3. Describe spatial or relational visual properties informally rather
   than using caption-like language.
4. Combine cues into approximate recollections rather than preserving
   their original wording.
5. Include mild uncertainty about a remembered detail.
6. Mention that one particular figure, diagram, or plot stood out.
7. Use ordinary first-person language rather than academic prose.

IMPORTANT:

Uncertainty may be expressed linguistically, but DO NOT create false
memories or alter the supplied facts.

Bad:
"Figure 3 shows the model's cross-attention mechanism..."

Better:
"I'm trying to find a paper I saw before. I remember one of the diagrams
had two parallel branches that came back together near the end..."

Return only the query.
